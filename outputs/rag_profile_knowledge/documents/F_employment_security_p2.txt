----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Facts that define the cluster itself. These variables and diagnostics produced the
cluster; they are NOT re-tested as post-cluster findings (doing so would be circular).

Branch: F — strict final-path analytical cohort n=81 (of 362 total strict-cohort respondents: D=191, E=34, F=81, G=56).
Retained dimension: Employment security and formality
Number of profiles in this retained model (k): 2
This profile's id and label: Profile 2 — "Predominantly permanent employment with stronger security"
This profile's exact frozen size: n=52 (cross-verified against the Part III freeze-verification table in the source report)

Exact cluster-forming variables (the only inputs used to build this clustering; all
other survey variables were deliberately excluded from distance calculation and are
only used descriptively in sections 2-3 below):
  - contract_type
  - job_security_12months
  - sector_formality

Theoretical basis and purpose: Employment arrangement formality and perceived 12-month job security (ILO SWTS employment-quality framework).

Clustering method: Gower distance over original mixed-type responses (no invented 0-100
scores), consensus of repeated K-medoids and spectral clustering across 100 matched
80% subsamples (200 base partitions), final clusters from K-medoids on (1 - co-association).

Validation diagnostics for this retained model: Gower silhouette 0.368; bootstrap/subsample ARI 0.953; source-method ARIs 0.854 and 0.950; 7 of 8 mandatory validation checks passed.
Final decision status: Retained with PAC/ambiguity caution.
Model-level caution (from the frozen decision register): Employment arrangement and contract satisfaction are related outcomes, not independent validation of the security/formality clusters.

Additional definitional context: This is the only retained Branch F clustering result. It describes employment conditions, not respondent worth or employability, and should guide questions about formalisation, certification, protection and feasible upgrading rather than automatically assigning a course. A related diagnostic (earnings/working-time preference, 'D4') was explicitly excluded from the final model set — it formed a stable but preference-controlled 51/30 split and is NOT part of this retained profile system.

Interpretation of this specific profile (verbatim from the frozen report's per-model
profile table):
  Permanent contracts and stronger expected security/formality were more common.

Reminder: this profile exists only WITHIN this one retained dimension. A respondent's
membership here says nothing about their membership in any other branch's or dimension's
profile system (see PROHIBITED_INFERENCES).

[Approved secondary conditional finding]
  - Approved secondary conditional finding (post-cluster, does not affect cluster assignment): Among the 63 of 81 Branch F respondents who reported working while studying, the two profiles differ in the TYPE of that prior work experience. Profile 1 (temporary/no-contract) was much more commonly associated with private-company internship/apprenticeship experience (17/22 eligible, 77.3%) than Profile 2 (12/41 eligible, 29.3%). Profile 2 (predominantly permanent) was more commonly associated with ordinary paid company work outside internships (23/41, 56.1%) than Profile 1 (3/22, 13.6%). Bias-corrected Cramer's V = 0.480; raw p = 0.0024; BH-adjusted p across the five targeted checks = 0.0119; sensitivity BH-adjusted p when appended to the existing F profiling family = 0.0492. This comparison is CONDITIONAL on the 63 respondents who worked while studying and does NOT apply to all 81 Branch F respondents. The two profiles did NOT meaningfully differ in WHETHER they worked while studying (22/29, 75.9% in Profile 1 vs 41/52, 78.8% in Profile 2). The work-type table was severely sparse (71.4% of expected cells below 5); only the two dominant categories above are interpreted, and rarer categories are not interpreted individually. This is an association, not evidence that earlier work type caused later employment security, and it does NOT mean Profile 1 internships were unpaid (paid work-while-studying status: 19/22, 86.4% in Profile 1 vs 39/41, 95.1% in Profile 2 — not a meaningful difference).

4. CAUTIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Model-level caution: Employment arrangement and contract satisfaction are related outcomes, not independent validation of the security/formality clusters.
Additional model context: This is the only retained Branch F clustering result. It describes employment conditions, not respondent worth or employability, and should guide questions about formalisation, certification, protection and feasible upgrading rather than automatically assigning a course. A related diagnostic (earnings/working-time preference, 'D4') was explicitly excluded from the final model set — it formed a stable but preference-controlled 51/30 split and is NOT part of this retained profile system.
FDR-correction context: Among the findings that passed the effect-size/coverage reporting screen for this model, 3 of 12 remained significant after FDR correction; 9 did not. (The separately-approved secondary conditional finding above did reach FDR-adjusted significance in its own targeted test family.)

Profile-specific caution (verbatim from the frozen evidence summary): Employment arrangement and contract satisfaction are related outcomes, not independent validation of the security/formality clusters. Some reported findings use eligible-subgroup denominators. Some categorical comparisons contain sparse cells. The work-type association applies only to the 63 respondents who worked while studying and has severe sparse cells (71.4% of expected cells below 5); it is non-causal and does not indicate that internships were unpaid.

Conditional-finding caution: the approved secondary work-while-studying finding in section 2 applies ONLY to the 63/81 Branch F respondents who reported working while studying, uses a severely sparse category table (71.4% of expected cells below 5), and does not extend to respondents who did not work while studying.

Conditional-denominator caution: 13 of 58 variables tested against this model are conditional (asked only of an eligible subgroup via survey routing, e.g. only active jobseekers, only those who worked while studying). Their percentages use the eligible-subgroup denominator shown in section 3, not the full profile size. A blank caused by survey routing was never treated as a 'No' answer during profiling.

Cross-dimensional caution: the following variables tested against this model also form part of another retained clustering model; their relationship to this profile is descriptive only and is NOT independent statistical validation of this model:
  - Employment arrangement: Employment arrangement can be associated with contract type.
  - Contract satisfaction: Closely related to contract type; interpret as an outcome rather than independent validation.
  - Recruitment method: Wage-worker subgroup only.
  - Preferred income/hours change: This item dominated a rejected D4 clustering and should be interpreted as a direct preference.
  - District: Many district categories can create sparse tables.
  - Business profitability: Self-employed subgroup only; n=11.
  - Gender equality at work: Wage-worker subgroup only.
  - Main business obstacle: Self-employed subgroup only; n=11.
  - Enterprise size: Wage-worker subgroup only.

Sparse-cell caution: 32 of 58 variables tested against this model contain sparse expected-cell counts (categorical tables with rare category combinations); percentages built from sparse tables are listed in section 3 and should be read with extra caution, especially for high-cardinality variables such as district, sector or stated obstacle.

General measurement cautions (apply to every profile in this knowledge base):
  - A reported percentage is a within-profile percentage in this 362-respondent strict
    cohort, not a population estimate for all Sri Lankan youth.
  - Effect sizes rank descriptive differences; they do not prove that cluster membership
    caused the characteristic.
  - Consensus clustering stability shows reproducibility under the specified procedure;
    it does not prove a natural, causal, or permanent youth type.
  - Profile names are descriptive and must not stigmatise respondents or be used to deny
    programme access.
