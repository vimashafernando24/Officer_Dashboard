Exact cluster-forming variables (the only inputs used to build this clustering; all
other survey variables were deliberately excluded from distance calculation and are
only used descriptively in sections 2-3 below):
  - household_income
  - household_size
  - household_unemployed
  - household_workers
  - primary_earner_status
  - primary_income_source

Theoretical basis and purpose: Household economic role/dependency position (Bourdieu economic capital / ILO SWTS household framework).

Clustering method: Gower distance over original mixed-type responses (no invented 0-100
scores), consensus of repeated K-medoids and spectral clustering across 100 matched
80% subsamples (200 base partitions), final clusters from K-medoids on (1 - co-association).

Validation diagnostics for this retained model: Gower silhouette 0.372; bootstrap/subsample ARI 1.000; source-method ARIs 0.854 and 0.718; permutation p=0.012; 9 of 9 mandatory validation checks passed.
Final decision status: Retained narrowly; describes role/dependency, not resource level.
Model-level caution (from the frozen decision register): This is an economic-role/dependency pattern, not a low-versus-high household-resource classification.

Additional definitional context: The original working title implied household resource LEVEL, but structural household-resource variables alone had ARI -0.016 with the final grouping — resource level does not explain this partition. Primary income source was the strongest single item, though no single item alone exactly reproduced membership. The defensible interpretation is therefore economic ROLE/DEPENDENCY (whether the respondent is a provider/contributor vs. supported by family/partner), not household wealth.

Interpretation of this specific profile (verbatim from the frozen report's per-model
profile table):
  86.7% were main/shared earners; 66.7% reported a regular job/business as their main personal income source.

Reminder: this profile exists only WITHIN this one retained dimension. A respondent's
membership here says nothing about their membership in any other branch's or dimension's
profile system (see PROHIBITED_INFERENCES).

Their percentages use the eligible-subgroup denominator shown in section 3, not the full profile size. A blank caused by survey routing was never treated as a 'No' answer during profiling.

Sparse-cell caution: 19 of 30 variables tested against this model contain sparse expected-cell counts (categorical tables with rare category combinations); percentages built from sparse tables are listed in section 3 and should be read with extra caution, especially for high-cardinality variables such as district, sector or stated obstacle.
